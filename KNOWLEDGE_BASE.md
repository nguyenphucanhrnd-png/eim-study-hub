## C1. OVERVIEW OF EXPORT & IMPORT MANAGEMENT

**Key points:** (1) Introduction of import & export management; (2) Export process; (3) Role of export & import management.

### 1.1 Definitions
- **Export:** In international trade, "exports" refers to selling goods produced in the home country to other markets.
- **Export management:** the application of the managerial process to the export function. It manages two things:
  1. **Outflow of goods** (physical flow)
  2. **Inflow of foreign exchange** (financial flow)
- Goal of export management: coordinate both flows efficiently so that **goods reach the buyer successfully & payment is received in full and on time**.
- Managed through: **Planning & Coordination, Documentation, Risk Management, Control & Monitoring**.

### 1.2 Physical flow — Export process (from seller's factory to foreign buyer), 9 steps
1. **Seller's factory** – goods produced and prepared for export
2. **Packing & loading at origin** – packed and loaded for delivery to port/airport
3. **Export customs clearance** (seller's country) – export declaration, document check, customs inspection, export release
4. **Port/airport of loading** – goods delivered to port/airport and loaded on board
5. **International transport** – by sea, air or other means to the buyer's country
6. **Port/airport of discharge** – goods arrive in the buyer's country
7. **Import customs clearance** (buyer's country) – import declaration, document check, customs inspection, **import duties & taxes payment**, customs release
8. **Inland transport to buyer** – to buyer's warehouse/final location
9. **Buyer's premises** – goods received by the buyer

### 1.3 Financial flow (inflow of foreign exchange)
A. **Buyer** pays according to the contract terms → B. **Buyer's bank** processes the payment and remits to the seller's bank → C. **Payment methods**: L/C, T/T, D/P, D/A or other agreed terms → D. **Seller's bank** receives the foreign exchange and credits the seller's account → E. **Seller** receives the foreign exchange (inflow of foreign currency).

### 1.4 Key documents
- **Key export documents (from seller):** Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Export Declaration, Bill of Lading / Air Waybill, Certificate of Origin (if required), Other Certificates (if required).
- **Key import documents (by buyer):** Import Declaration, B/L / AWB, Commercial Invoice, Packing List, C/O (if required), Other Certificates (if required).

### 1.5 Effective coordination of physical and financial flow
**Right goods → Right place → Right time → Right documents → Right payment → Acceptable risk**

How each course topic maps to the two flows:
- Planning & Preparation → **plan** the two flows
- Pricing & Incoterms® → **allocate cost/risk** in the goods flow
- Cargo Insurance → **protect** the goods flow
- International Payment → **manage** the financial flow
- International Sale Contract → **legally connect** the two flows
- Documents & Procedures → **enable and control** both flows

### 1.6 Essential elements of the export process (6)
1. Get ready to export
2. Plan for market entry strategy
3. Start finding foreign buyers
4. Determine terms of sale and terms of payment
5. Identify the steps required to successfully ship goods and complete the transaction
6. Research and confirm duties, taxes, shipping and insurance costs

### 1.7 Export order process (4 stages)
**Quotation (price quotation) → Order entry → Shipment → Collection**

### 1.8 Interrelationships with outside service providers (Figure 1-6)
At the center is the **Vice President (Director) Export/Import Operations Department**. It connects with:
- Treasury or Accounting Department / Banks
- Information Systems, Legal, Manufacturing
- VP Marketing (Sales) → Customers
- VP Purchasing → Suppliers
- Transportation Carriers, Freight Forwarders, Customs Brokers
- Insurance Companies and Sureties, Packing Companies
- Government Agencies, Translators, Consulates, Preshipment Inspection Companies

### 1.9 Players in an international trade transaction (12)
Exporter; Importer; Banks; Port authorities/companies; Carrier/transporter; Freight forwarder; Customs; Clearing agent; Insurance companies; Inspection companies; Arbitrator; Chamber of Commerce.

### 1.10 Role of export & import management (6)
1. **Facilitating international trade:** access international markets; expand the customer base and supplier networks; develop cross-border business activities.
2. **Coordinating international trade operations:** procurement; sales & marketing; logistics & transportation; customs authorities; banks & financial institutions; carriers, shipping lines, freight forwarders; insurance companies; international customers; government agencies. → *The Export Manager acts as the coordinator* who ensures effective communication and collaboration among all parties.
3. **Managing risks:** identify, assess and minimize: payment risk, foreign exchange risk, transportation risk, legal & contractual risk, political risk, documentary risk.
4. **Creating customer value:** customers buy not only products but also reliable, professional services: on-time delivery, accurate documentation, secure and convenient payment, responsive after-sales service → satisfaction, trust, long-term relationships.
5. **Enhancing organizational competitiveness:** reduce logistics & operating costs; shorten delivery lead times; minimize documentation errors; improve responsiveness; enhance competitive advantage → operational excellence, sustainable growth.
6. **Enabling digital trade transformation:** AI for export market research & demand forecasting; Big Data analytics to identify/evaluate international market opportunities; electronic trade documentation (e-Invoice, e-Bill of Lading, e-Certificate of Origin, e-Customs); ERP integration with logistics & finance; data analytics for managerial decision-making.

## C2. PLANNING AND PREPARATIONS FOR EXPORT

**Key points:** Export Plan; Export Readiness.

### 2.1 Export plan — definition & 9 components
An **Export Plan** is a structured proposal for exporting **a specific product to a specific target market**.
1. Export objectives
2. Target market and target customer
3. Market entry / Distribution
4. Product for foreign market
5. Pricing & Payment
6. Logistics & Delivery
7. Resources & Responsibilities
8. Financial analysis
9. Risk management

### 2.2 Export objectives & KPIs
- Typical objectives: enter a new foreign market; increase export sales; develop new international customers; expand the customer base.
- KPIs:
  - **Export Sales Revenue** – total value of export sales
  - **Export Sales Growth (%)** – percentage increase in export sales
  - **Gross Profit Margin (%)** – percentage of revenue remaining after export-related costs
  - **Expected Profit (%)** – expected profit relative to export revenue
  - **Break-even Point** – minimum sales revenue required to cover total costs
  - **Loss (%)** – maximum acceptable loss (%) under an unfavorable scenario

### 2.3 Target market & target customer
- **Market opportunity:** market size, market growth, market trends and demand.
- **Market attractiveness & accessibility:** competition (key competitors), regulations, trade barriers (tariff & non-tariff barriers), **Technical Barriers to Trade (TBT)**, logistics connectivity.
- **Target customer:** B2B/B2C, customer segment, purchasing needs & behavior; customer needs, product requirements, value proposition; **Target Customer Profile** (company size, industry, geographic location, distribution network, financial capability, …).

### 2.4 Market entry / Distribution
- How do we reach the target customer?
  - **Direct export** → direct relationship with the foreign buyer
  - **Indirect export** → importer / distributor / agent / retailer
  - **Cross-border e-commerce** → online marketplace / D2C (direct-to-consumer)
- The channel choice influences product design, packaging, logistics and delivery:
  - B2B importer/distributor → bulk supply, packaging designed mainly for transport & B2B handling
  - Supermarket/retail → consumer-oriented packaging, labeling, barcodes
  - Cross-border e-commerce → e-commerce-friendly packaging, fulfillment solutions, individual-unit packaging, reliable last-mile delivery

### 2.5 Product for foreign market
- Product selection & development: select current products for new markets; modify current products or develop new ones; product–market–customer fit; product characteristics (design, styles, colors…), quality, product standards (health & safety); packaging, labeling, markings, language; product life cycle, seasonality, shelf life.
- **Foreign intellectual property protection:** brand name, trademarks, trade secrets, patents, industrial designs, copyrights.

### 2.6 Pricing & Payment
- How is the export price determined (export price, cost & margin, pricing strategy)?
- Incoterms (11 rules): EXW, FCA, FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP – allocation of costs and risks.
- Exporters should specify the **currency** in which the price is quoted.
- Payment terms: payment method; **payment timing** (prepayment, payment at sight, deferred payment); payment currency; payment risk.

### 2.7 Logistics & Delivery
1. Delivery & transportation: order processing & delivery timelines (lead time, delivery schedules); coordination between production, inventory and shipment; transport mode selection (sea, air, road, rail, multimodal); shipment scheduling.
2. Warehousing & inventory: storage requirements; inventory control methods.
3. Cargo insurance: freight/cargo insurance requirements.
4. Logistics intermediaries: freight forwarders, customs brokers, shipping lines/carriers.
5. Documentation.

### 2.8 Resources & Responsibilities
Who executes the plan: Export & Import Dept, Sales/Marketing, Production, Finance/Accounting, Logistics, Documentation, Quality Control, external partners. → **Export management is cross-functional management.**

### 2.9 Financial analysis
- **Export Revenue = Export Quantity × Export Price.** Example: 18 tons × EUR 2,800/ton = **EUR 50,400**.
- **Export/Import costs:**
  - Product costs: production cost, packaging, handling, documentation, other export-related costs
  - Logistics costs: inland transportation, freight, loading/unloading, port charges, other logistics costs
  - Duties & taxes (where applicable): import duties, export-related taxes, other taxes/fees
- **Gross Profit = Revenue − COGS**; **Gross Profit Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue × 100%**
- **Foreign exchange considerations:** current rate; deferred payment (e.g., 3 or 6 months); exchange-rate fluctuations; impact on revenue, costs and profitability. *The agreed foreign-currency amount may remain unchanged, but its value in the home currency may change over time.*
- **FX example (slide):** a Vietnamese company exports coffee to a European buyer; payment 6 months after receiving the goods; value EUR 50,000; at contract date 1 EUR = 30,000 VND → expected **VND 1.50 billion**.
  - Scenario 1 – EUR depreciates to 28,000 → VND 1.40 billion → **loss VND 100 million**
  - Scenario 2 – EUR appreciates to 32,000 → VND 1.60 billion → **gain VND 100 million**
  - Remark: EUR depreciation → lower VND revenue → lower profitability; EUR appreciation → the opposite. The buyer still pays EUR 50,000; only the VND value changes.

### 2.10 Risk management (6 groups)
- **Transportation risk:** loss/damage in transit; partial or total loss; delays.
- **Foreign credit / payment risk:** non-payment; delayed payment; buyer's creditworthiness.
- **Foreign exchange risk:** depreciation of the buyer's currency against the exporter's currency before payment; FX fluctuations affecting profitability; inability to obtain/transfer foreign currency in the buyer's country.
- **Market risk:** changes in demand, market conditions; unexpected changes in competition or prices.
- **Regulatory & compliance risk:** changes in import regulations; product standards; customs/documentation problems; non-compliance with destination-market requirements.
- **Political / country risk:** political instability; trade restrictions or sanctions; changes in government policy; economic or geopolitical disruptions.

### 2.11 Preparation of export — the 11 questions
1. Which products are selected for export development, and what modifications are needed to adapt them for overseas markets?
2. Is an export license needed?
3. Which countries are targeted for sales development?
4. In each country, what are the basic customer profiles, and which marketing and distribution channels should be used?
5. What special challenges pertain to each market (competition, cultural differences, import/export controls), and what strategy addresses them?
6. How will the product's export sales price be determined?
7. What specific operational steps must be taken and when?
8. What is the time frame for implementing each element of the plan?
9. What personnel and company resources will be dedicated to exporting?
10. What will be the cost in time and money for each element?
11. How will results be evaluated and used to modify the plan?

## C3. PRICING IN INTERNATIONAL TRADE & INCOTERMS

**Key points:** Pricing considerations; approaches to export pricing; scope & structure of Incoterms; export pricing under terms of sale.

### 3.1 Six approaches to export pricing
1. **Cost-based pricing** – export price based on full cost plus markup, or full cost plus a desired return on investment.
2. **Marginal pricing** – price does **not** cover total cost; it includes only the **marginal (variable) cost** of producing the product for the export market.
3. **Skimming pricing** – charging a **premium** price.
4. **Penetration pricing** – charging **lower** prices to increase market share.
5. **Demand-based pricing** – based on what buyers are willing to pay; set by market demand.
6. **Competitive pricing** – based on competitive pressures in the market.

### 3.2 Pricing objectives → pricing strategy (diagram)
| Objectives | Price level | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Maximize sales; increase market share; convey an image of discounts | **Minimum price**; **same price for all markets** | **Penetration pricing; Cost-based pricing** |
| Achieve a desired profit level; convey an image of prestige | **Maximum price**; **different prices for different markets** | **Price skimming; Demand-based pricing** |

### 3.3 What are Incoterms?
- A set of rules for the **interpretation of trade terms** in a contract of sale.
- **Incoterms are not law.** They are the **ICC (International Chamber of Commerce)** official rules.
- History: first published by ICC in **1936**; amended in 1953, 1967, 1976, 1980, 1990, 2000, 2010 and **2020**.
- **Purpose:** (1) identify the obligations of seller and buyer; (2) division of costs and expenses; (3) transfer of risk (the point where risk of loss/damage passes); (4) help the exporter calculate the export price under the chosen rule.
- **Scope – Incoterms DO NOT cover:** (1) **transfer of title** (ownership); (2) **payment terms or methods**; (3) **breach of contract** (remedies, liabilities, consequences).
- Key point: Incoterms regulate the division of costs, risks and responsibilities related to **delivery of goods only**; they do not govern the contract itself.

### 3.4 Structure of Incoterms 2010 (4 groups)
- **Group E – Departure:** EXW
- **Group F – Main carriage unpaid:** FCA, FAS, FOB
- **Group C – Main carriage paid:** CFR, CIF, CPT, CIP
- **Group D – Arrival:** DAT, DAP, DDP

### 3.5 Incoterms 2020 – 2 groups by mode of transport
- **Rules for any mode or modes of transport (7):** EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP
- **Rules for sea and inland waterway transport (4):** FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF
- (E/F/C/D grouping still applies to 2020: E = EXW; F = FCA, FAS, FOB; C = CPT, CIP, CFR, CIF; D = DAP, DPU, DDP.)

### 3.6 Main differences between Incoterms® 2010 and 2020 (7)
| # | Area | 2010 | 2020 | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rule names | DAT – Delivered at Terminal | **DPU – Delivered at Place Unloaded** | The only name change among the 11 rules; destination need not be a terminal; seller delivers after unloading at the named place |
| 2 | FCA & on-board B/L | Did not address obtaining an on-board B/L | Parties may agree that the **buyer instructs its carrier to issue an on-board B/L to the seller** | Helps the seller obtain an on-board B/L (e.g., to meet L/C requirements) |
| 3 | Insurance | CIF/CIP less differentiated | **CIF: minimum ICC (C)**; **CIP: ICC (A)** or equivalent | CIF = lower default cover; CIP = higher default cover |
| 4 | Own means of transport | Not expressly addressed | Expressly recognized under **FCA, DAP, DPU, DDP** | Parties may use their own vehicles |
| 5 | Security requirements | Less prominent | Incorporated more clearly into carriage & customs obligations | Reflects supply-chain security |
| 6 | Costs presentation | Scattered across provisions | **Consolidated in A9/B9** | Easier to see who pays what |
| 7 | Guidance | Guidance Notes | **Explanatory Notes for Users** + improved visuals | Easier to understand and apply |

### 3.7 Pre-carriage, main-carriage, on-carriage
- **Pre-carriage:** inland transportation on the seller's side from the shipment origin to the port of departure.
- **Main-carriage:** transportation from the seller's side to the buyer's side (international freight).
- **On-carriage (post-carriage):** transportation from the arrival point on the buyer's side to any other place (e.g., buyer's premises).
- Points along the chain: seller's premises → packaging → loading → export customs clearance → Terminal Handling Charges (THC) at origin → main transport → THC at destination → import customs clearance → unloading → buyer's premises.

### 3.8 The 11 rules — obligation matrix (per slides)

| Rule | Full name | Mode | Named place | Export licence | Import licence | Main carriage contract | Insurance obligation | Delivery = risk transfer point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EXW** | Ex Works | Any | Named place of delivery | **Buyer** | Buyer | Buyer | None | Goods placed at buyer's disposal at named place (factory/warehouse; may or may not be seller's premises), **not loaded** |
| **FCA** | Free Carrier | Any | Named place of delivery | Seller | Buyer | Buyer | None | Delivered to the carrier: **at seller's premises → once loaded on the means of transport**; **any other place → on seller's means of transport, ready for unloading** |
| **FAS** | Free Alongside Ship | Sea | Named **port of shipment** | Seller | Buyer | Buyer | None | Placed **alongside the ship** (on quay or barge) nominated by the buyer at the named port, or procures goods so delivered |
| **FOB** | Free On Board | Sea | Named **port of shipment** | Seller | Buyer | Buyer | None | **On board the vessel** nominated by the buyer at the named port, or procures goods so delivered |
| **CFR** | Cost and Freight | Sea | Named **port of destination** | Seller | Buyer | **Seller** | None | **On board the vessel at port of shipment** |
| **CIF** | Cost, Insurance and Freight | Sea | Named **port of destination** | Seller | Buyer | **Seller** | **Seller – minimum ICC (C)** | **On board the vessel at port of shipment** |
| **CPT** | Carriage Paid To | Any | Named **place of destination** | Seller | Buyer | **Seller** | None | Delivered to the **first carrier** |
| **CIP** | Carriage and Insurance Paid To | Any | Named **place of destination** | Seller | Buyer | **Seller** | **Seller – ICC (A)** | Delivered to the **first carrier** |
| **DAP** | Delivered at Place | Any | Named place of destination | Seller | Buyer | Seller | None | At disposal of buyer **on the arriving means of transport, ready for unloading**, at named place |
| **DPU** | Delivered at Place Unloaded | Any | Named place of destination | Seller | Buyer | Seller | None | **Once unloaded** from the arriving means of transport, at named place |
| **DDP** | Delivered Duty Paid | Any | Named place of destination | **Seller** | **Seller** | Seller | None | At disposal of buyer, **cleared for import**, on arriving means **ready for unloading**, at named place |

**General rule in every slide table:** the seller pays costs until the goods are delivered; the buyer pays costs from delivery. Risk passes from seller to buyer **at delivery**.

**Key insights to teach (derived directly from the table):**
- **EXW** = minimum obligation for the seller (buyer handles even export clearance). **DDP** = maximum obligation for the seller (seller handles even import clearance).
- **C-group rules have two critical points:** **risk** passes at the **origin** (on board / first carrier), while the seller pays **main carriage costs** up to the **destination**. Classic trap: "CIF Hamburg" ≠ risk transfers at Hamburg.
- **FOB / CFR / CIF** – delivery = on board the vessel at the port of shipment. **FAS** – alongside the ship.
- **DPU is the only rule where the seller must unload at destination.**
- Only **CIF and CIP** impose an insurance obligation (on the seller). Under other rules, the party bearing the risk may insure at its own discretion (C6, Art. 9).
- FAS/FOB/CFR/CIF are for **sea and inland waterway only**; for containers handed over at an inland terminal, the multimodal equivalents are FCA/CPT/CIP. ([EXT] ICC Incoterms® 2020 Explanatory Notes recommend FCA instead of FOB for containerised goods.)
- Correct notation: **Rule + named place + version**, e.g., "CIF Hamburg, Germany – Incoterms® 2020" (see C6).

## C4. EXPORT AND IMPORT CARGO INSURANCE

**Key points:** (1) Risks in foreign trade; (2) Marine insurance.

### 4.1 Four major types of risks in foreign trade
- **Political risk** – government actions, war, revolution, terrorism and strikes that disrupt international business and may result in cargo confiscation.
- **Foreign credit risk** – non-payment or delayed payment by foreign buyers.
- **Transportation risk** – partial or total loss of, or damage to, goods during transit.
- **Foreign exchange / transfer risk** – depreciation of the buyer's currency against the exporter's currency before payment, or inability to obtain/transfer foreign currency for payment in the buyer's country.

### 4.2 Marine (cargo) insurance
- Provides **financial protection against loss of or damage to goods in transit caused by insured risks**; in return, the insured pays an **insurance premium**.
- It is a **contract of indemnity**: the insurer compensates the insured for loss/damage caused by insured risks, as specified in the policy.

### 4.3 Two essential principles of insurance
1. **Insurable interest** (Nguyên tắc quyền lợi được bảo hiểm): a financial interest based on some legal right in the preservation of the insured property.
2. **Subrogation** (Nguyên tắc thế quyền): on paying the insured's claim, the insurer **stands in the position of the insured** to claim from other parties responsible for the loss or damage (e.g., the carrier).

### 4.4 Institute Cargo Clauses (ICC)
| | ICC (A) | ICC (B) | ICC (C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage | **Widest** | Medium | Basic / narrow |
| Approach | Broad coverage, **subject to exclusions** ("all risks") | **Named perils**, broader than C | **Named perils** |
| Relative level | Highest | Medium | Lowest |

## C5. INTERNATIONAL PAYMENT

**Key points:** procedures of payment methods: Consignment sale; Open account; Remittance (T/T); Documentary Collection (DC); Letter of Credit (L/C).

### 5.1 Consignment sale
- The exporter sends the product to an importer on a **deferred-payment basis**: the importer pays only **after the goods are sold to a third party**.
- **Title passes to the importer only when payment is made to the exporter.**
- Problems (4): delays in payment; risk of non-payment; cost of returning merchandise; limited sales effort by importers.

### 5.2 Open account
- The exporter ships goods to the overseas customer **on credit**; payment within an agreed period.
- The seller ships the goods and **separately mails the shipping documents** to the buyer.
- Payment terms range from **30 to 120 days** after the shipping invoice date or receipt of goods, depending on the country.

### 5.3 Remittance – T/T (Telegraphic Transfer)
Three forms: **payment in advance; payment at sight; deferred payment.**
- **Payment in advance (Cash-in-Advance)** flow: buyer instructs its bank to remit → importer's bank transfers to exporter's bank → exporter's bank credits the exporter → **exporter ships the goods last**.
  - Risks for **buyer**: loses use of funds until goods arrive; seller may not ship as ordered (quantity, product, quality, shipping method); seller may not ship when requested.
  - When to use cash-in-advance (4): the importer is a new customer and/or has a less-established operating history; the importer's creditworthiness is doubtful, unsatisfactory or unverifiable; the political and commercial risks of the importer's country are very high; the exporter's product is unique, not available elsewhere, or in heavy demand.
- **Deferred payment** flow: (1) seller ships goods to buyer → (2) buyer instructs its bank → (3) importer's bank confirms/debits the buyer → (4) importer's bank remits to exporter's bank → (5) exporter's bank pays the exporter. → **Goods go first, money later → risk on the exporter.**
- (Flow numbering is reconstructed from the diagrams; see ERRATA E-09.)

### 5.4 Documentary Collection (DC)
- A **service offered by banks** to sellers to facilitate payment. The exporter **draws a draft on the buyer after shipment**, requesting payment on presentation of documents (**D/P**) or acceptance of the draft to pay at a future determinable date (**D/A**).
- **D/P – Documents against Payment:** the buyer receives the shipping documents **only after making payment** at the bank.
- **D/A – Documents against Acceptance:** the buyer receives the title documents **after signing (accepting) the time draft**, promising to pay later.
- **Bill of Exchange / Draft:** an **unconditional written order** drawn by one person (**drawer**) to another (**drawee**) requiring the drawee to pay on demand or at a fixed/determinable future time a stated sum to the order of a specified person.
  - Drawer = exporter/seller; Drawee = importer/buyer; Beneficiary = seller/drawer or another person.
- **Two types of drafts:**
  - **Sight draft** – payable upon presentation to the drawee (→ D/P).
  - **Time draft / Usance draft** – payment due within a certain time after the buyer accepts the draft and receives the goods (→ D/A).
- **Procedure (Figure 11.2) – 7 steps:**
  1. Agreement on terms of sale and payment; seller ships goods and prepares documents
  2. Seller presents documents to the **remitting bank** (seller's bank)
  3. Remitting bank forwards documents to the **collecting bank** (buyer's bank)
  4. Collecting bank presents documents to the buyer
  5. Buyer accepts or pays on presentation of documents to the collecting bank
  6. Collecting bank advises the remitting bank of acceptance or remits payment
  7. Remitting bank advises the seller of acceptance or remits payment
- **Risks:** high risk for the seller – **no bank guarantee of payment**; no protection against order cancellation; seller is paid only after shipping; buyer's risk – goods shipped might not conform to specifications.
- The seller should agree to DC if (4): no doubt about the buyer's ability and willingness to pay; the buyer's country is politically, economically and legally stable; no exchange restrictions in the buyer's country; the shipped goods are easily marketable.
- [EXT] Documentary collections are usually subject to **URC 522** (ICC Uniform Rules for Collections).

### 5.5 Letter of Credit (Documentary Credit)
- **Definition:** a **bank's promise to pay** a seller on behalf of the buyer, provided the seller meets the terms and conditions stated in the credit.
  - **Documents are the key issue** in L/C transactions.
  - **Banks act as intermediaries and have nothing to do with the goods themselves.**
- **Parties:** Buyer = **Applicant**; Seller = **Beneficiary**; Applicant's bank = **Issuing bank / Opening bank**; Beneficiary's bank = **Advising bank**, or **Confirming bank / Paying bank**.
- **Basic procedure (9 steps, per slide):**
  1. Buyer applies for and opens the L/C with the issuing bank
  2. Issuing bank issues the L/C, forwarding it to the advising bank
  3. Advising bank notifies the seller of the L/C
  4. Seller delivers the goods once the terms of the L/C have been met
  5. Seller forwards the documents stipulated in the L/C to the advising bank
  6. Advising bank forwards the documents to the issuing bank for review
  7. Issuing bank reviews, accepts and pays the seller's bank (advising bank)
  8. Issuing bank forwards the documents to the buyer; the buyer pays or the buyer's account is debited
  9. Advising bank pays the seller as specified in the L/C
- Figure 11.3 (US seller in New York – Canadian buyer in Montreal) also shows the **Carrier**: the seller delivers goods to the carrier; the buyer presents documents (B/L) to the carrier to receive the goods.
- **Types of L/C (11):** 1 Revocable; 2 Irrevocable; 3 Irrevocable confirmed; 4 Irrevocable without recourse; 5 Back-to-back; 6 Transferable; 7 Revolving; 8 Standby; 9 Red clause; 10 Deferred payment; 11 Reciprocal.
  - (The slides only list the names. Definitions for explanations [EXT, general knowledge]: *confirmed* = a second bank (confirming bank) adds its own payment undertaking; *transferable* = the beneficiary may transfer all or part to a second beneficiary; *back-to-back* = a second L/C issued on the strength of the first; *revolving* = automatically reinstated for repeated shipments; *red clause* = allows an advance to the beneficiary before shipment; *standby* = a guarantee, paid only if the applicant defaults; *reciprocal* = used in counter-trade/processing, effective only when the counter-L/C is opened; *deferred payment* = payment at a future date without a draft.)
- **Contents of an L/C (13):** 1 Issuing bank; 2 L/C number; 3 Place & date of issue; 4 Type of L/C; 5 Beneficiary; 6 Amount; 7 Expiry date; 8 Description of goods; 9 Required documents; 10 Terms of sale; 11 Terms and conditions of delivery; 12 Commitment of issuing bank; 13 Signature of issuing bank.
  - **Validity period** = from the **issue date** to the **expiry date**.
- **Remarks on dates (meaning per slide, rephrased; see ERRATA E-06):**
  - **Issue date** must be **before** the shipment date.
  - **Shipment date** must fall **within the validity period** (before the expiry date).
  - **Expiry date** must be **after** the shipment date.
  - Documents must be presented **no later than 21 days after the shipment date** and **within the validity period** of the L/C.
  - **If buyer and seller agree to extend the shipment date, the expiry date is extended too. Conversely, if the expiry date is extended, the shipment date is NOT extended.**
  - [EXT] UCP 600 Art. 14(c): presentation **no later than 21 calendar days after shipment**, and **in any event no later than the expiry date**. UCP 600 Art. 29(c): an expiry date extended because the bank was closed does not extend the latest shipment date.
- **Discrepancies (3 types):**
  - **Accidental** – easily corrected by the beneficiary or the issuing bank.
  - **Minor** – corrected by a **written waiver from the buyer**.
  - **Major** – cannot be corrected, or corrected only by an **amendment to the L/C**.
- **Exporter's L/C checklist (14):**
  1. All names and addresses are correct
  2. No unacceptable conditions
  3. Documents can be obtained in the required form
  4. Unit price and total price conform
  5. Cost of goods and other charges in the sales agreement comply with the L/C amount
  6. Partial shipments / transshipments are specified correctly
  7. Description of goods consistent between the L/C and the commercial invoice
  8. Shipping, expiration and presentation dates allow sufficient time to process the order, ship, and prepare documents
  9. Points of dispatch, taking in charge, loading on board, or discharge are as agreed
  10. Freight payment indicated properly
  11. Instructions on whom drafts are drawn and their tenor are correct
  12. Whether drafts may be less than 100% of invoice value
  13. Insurance coverage and the party paying charges are as agreed
  14. All required documents need to be presented

[EXT – teaching aid] Risk ladder for the **exporter** (lowest → highest risk): Cash-in-advance → L/C (confirmed > unconfirmed) → D/P → D/A → Open account → Consignment. Reversed for the importer. (Source: U.S. Department of Commerce/ITA, *Trade Finance Guide*.)

## C6. INTERNATIONAL SALE CONTRACT

**Key points:** main articles of an international sale contract; mistakes/errors in an international sale contract.

### 6.1 Definition & parties
- An **International Sale Contract (ISC)** is an agreement that regulates the rights and obligations of the parties regarding the sale and purchase of products.
- **Seller:** provides the goods, transfers documents of title, collects the money. **Buyer:** makes payment and receives the goods.

### 6.2 Structure (3 phases)
- **Phase 1 – Contracting parties:** name of contract; contract number; date & place signed; contracting parties (name, address, tel, fax, represented by, "hereinafter called the Seller/Buyer"); terminology (if necessary).
- **Phase 2 – Main articles (14):** 1 Commodity; 2 Quality; 3 Quantity; 4 Packing & Marking; 5 Price; 6 Delivery; 7 Payment; 8 Documents; 9 Insurance; 10 Claim; 11 Penalty; 12 Arbitration; 13 Force Majeure; 14 Other terms & conditions.
- **Phase 3 – Signatures** (For the Seller / For the Buyer).

### 6.3 Art. 1 – Commodity
Goods should be **simply and exactly described**: technical and commercial name; name of manufacturer; country of origin; etc. Simple case → listed in the body; complex case → description in an **appendix**.

### 6.4 Art. 2 – Quality (5 methods)
1. **By samples** – the seller sends samples to the buyer, or the buyer sends samples to the seller.
2. **By standard** – name of issuing organization; standard number/code; date/year/version; grade/classification if applicable.
3. **By detailed description** – size & dimensions, material & composition, grade, color, design & model, performance, technical specifications, tolerance, other requirements.
4. **By trademark/brand** – brand, model, product code, version. Example: *Samsung Galaxy S25, Model SM-S931B*.
5. **By technical documents** – catalogue, technical specifications, product data sheet, manufacturing drawings, technical standards; specify version, date, ID number where necessary.

### 6.5 Art. 3 – Quantity
- Exact quantity: e.g., 500 metric tons; 10,000 pieces; 2,000 cartons.
- Units: **MT = 1,000 kg; LT (long ton) = 1,016.05 kg; ST (short ton) = 907.18 kg**; lb; meter; **yard = 0.914 m**; **mile ≈ 1.609 km** ([ERRATA E-07]: the slide writes "1,609 km").
- **Tolerance** (approximate quantity): ±5%; "5% more or less at the seller's option"; e.g., *Rice: 1,000 MT more or less 5% at the Buyer's option*.
- **Method of determination:** net weight; gross weight; per weight certificate issued by an independent surveyor.

### 6.6 Art. 4 – Packing & Marking
Specify the required packaging: materials; sizes & shapes (cases, bales, drums, rolls, bags); package interiors; volume and weight of packages to save transport costs.

### 6.7 Art. 5 – Price (5 elements)
1. **Currency of price** – prefer widely accepted, convertible currencies (USD, EUR) to reduce FX risk; if price currency ≠ payment currency → specify the exchange rate and the date for determining it.
2. **Trade term – Incoterms®:** specify **rule + version + named place/port**, e.g., *CIF Hamburg, Germany – Incoterms® 2020*.
3. **Unit price:** e.g., *USD 500/MT, CIF Hamburg, Germany – Incoterms® 2020*.
4. **Total contract value** in figures and words: *USD 250,000 – Say: US Dollars Two Hundred and Fifty Thousand Only.*
5. **Price adjustment** (if applicable): for long-term contracts or volatile prices → fixed price or adjustable price.

### 6.8 Art. 6 – Delivery
- 6.1 **Term of delivery:** Incoterms rule + version + named place; determines the allocation of costs, risks, responsibilities.
- 6.2 **Time of delivery:** a **fixed date** (e.g., shipped on Sept 20, 2026) or a **period** (between Aug 1 and Sept 30, 2026). **Do NOT use general expressions:** "subject to shipping space available", "subject to the opening of L/C", "prompt", "immediately", "as soon as possible".
- 6.3 **Place of delivery** per the Incoterms rule: port of shipment/loading; port of destination/discharge; port of transshipment; place of receipt/departure; place of destination.
- 6.4 **Notice of delivery:** who gives notice; when; method; content (vessel name, mode of transport, port of loading, port of destination, shipment date, **ETD, ETA**); before and/or after shipment.
- 6.5 **Others:** partial shipment allowed/not allowed; total shipment/shipment in one lot; transshipment allowed/prohibited; shipping documents, loading requirements, inspection at delivery, special handling/storage.
- **Slide example:** FOB Saigon Port, Vietnam – Incoterms® 2020; shipped **no later than Aug 30, 2026**; place of delivery: Saigon Port; port of discharge: Osaka Port, Japan; partial shipment and transshipment not allowed. **Pre-shipment advice:** the **Buyer** advises the Seller of the carrying vessel's name **≥3 days before** the scheduled shipment date by email (logical, since under FOB the buyer books the vessel). **Final shipment advice:** **within 24 hours after loading**, the **Seller** advises the Buyer by email: contract no., quantity, gross weight, net weight, measurement, number of packages, invoice value, vessel name, B/L number. Notices go to the designated emails; the sender requests confirmation of receipt.

### 6.9 Art. 7 – Payment
- 7.1 **Currency of payment** (same as the price currency? if not → exchange rate & method). E.g., payment in USD.
- 7.2 **Method of payment:** T/T, D/A, D/P, L/C – study the risks carefully; where applicable specify the banks, required documents, applicable rules (**UCP 600** for documentary credits), bank charges & who bears them. Example: *irrevocable documentary L/C at sight, subject to UCP 600*.
- 7.3 **Time of payment:** in advance (before shipment); at sight (upon presentation of complying documents); deferred (at a future date or within a period after an agreed event). Examples: *60 days from the B/L date*; *within 30 days after receipt of the required documents*.
- **Example:** irrevocable L/C at sight for 100% of invoice value; issuing bank: Tokyo Commercial Bank; notifying bank: Vietcombank HCMC; each party bears bank charges in its own country; **amendment/extension charges borne by the party requesting them**.

### 6.10 Art. 8 – Documents
- The seller provides the documents required by the contract, regulations and payment terms: Commercial Invoice; Bill of Lading; Certificate of Quantity; Certificate of Quality; Packing List; Insurance Certificate/Policy; Certificate of Origin; others (Inspection, Phytosanitary, Health, Fumigation Certificate; Dangerous Goods Declaration).
- **Example (Payment & Documents):** irrevocable L/C at sight, 100% invoice value, in favor of the Seller; documents presented **within 20 days after the shipment date**: signed commercial invoice ×2; **full set 3/3 original clean on-board B/L, made out to order of the L/C opening bank, marked Freight Prepaid, notify the Buyer**; detailed packing list ×2; certificate of quantity & quality issued by the manufacturer ×2; **C/O issued by VCCI**.

### 6.11 Art. 9 – Insurance
- Responsibility depends on Incoterms: **under CIF & CIP the seller arranges and pays for cargo insurance**; under other rules, the party bearing the risk may insure at its discretion unless otherwise agreed.
- Coverage to specify: conditions (ICC A/B/C…); **insured amount (e.g., 110% of contract/CIF value)**; currency; insured risks; claims payable at (destination).

### 6.12 Art. 10 – Claim
1. **Grounds:** discrepancy in quantity (damage/shortfall) or quality; other non-conformities.
2. **Claim period:** length and starting date of defects liability; claims within [X] days/months from the agreed start date; **latent defects** within [X] from the date of discovery, within the defects liability period; after the period, the seller is no longer liable.
3. **Claim documents:** Letter of Claim, Survey Report, Invoice, B/L, Certificate of Quantity, Certificate of Quality, Packing List, Report on Receipt of Cargo, Cargo Outturn Report.

### 6.13 Art. 11 – Penalty
1. **Late delivery** (except force majeure): seller pays [X]% of the **value of undelivered goods** per day of delay, capped at [Y]%.
2. **Late payment** (except force majeure/agreed circumstances): buyer pays [X]% of the **overdue unpaid amount** per day, capped at [Y]%.
3. **Limitation:** total penalty ≤ the agreed maximum.

### 6.14 Art. 12 – Arbitration
1. **Applicable law:** CISG; or the law of the seller's country; or the law of the buyer's country. Variants: CISG + seller's law (for matters not governed by CISG); CISG + buyer's law.
2. **Dispute settlement:** by arbitration unless otherwise agreed (including disputes about existence, validity, termination).
3. **Place of arbitration:** [City, Country].
4. **Arbitration costs:** borne by seller / buyer / per the award.
- **CISG** (United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods): adopted **11 April 1980 in Vienna**; entered into force **1 January 1988**; **Vietnam acceded on 18 December 2015**; **in force for Vietnam from 1 January 2017**; purpose: a uniform legal framework for international sale-of-goods contracts.

### 6.15 Art. 13 – Force majeure
- Events that are **unforeseeable, unavoidable and impossible to overcome**.
- Typical list: war, riot, insurrection, strikes, lockout, government regulations, fire, explosion, flood, storm, earthquake, other abnormal natural events.
- **Notification** of force majeure by the parties.
- **Resolution:** **terminate** the contract or **prolong** the performance period.
- Neither party is liable for failure/delay to the extent caused by force majeure.

### 6.16 Art. 14 – Other terms & conditions
1. **Amendments:** valid only in writing and signed by both parties.
2. **Assignment:** no transfer of rights/obligations to a third party without prior written consent.
3. **Entire agreement:** supersedes all previous negotiations/correspondence; notices in writing to the specified addresses/emails.
4. **Language:** English [and Vietnamese]; **if discrepant, the English version prevails**.
5. **Effectiveness:** in force from the date of signing by both parties.

### 6.17 Common contract mistakes (derived from the "should/should not" recommendations in the slides)
- Incoterms rule without version/named place (e.g., only "CIF").
- Vague delivery time ("prompt", "ASAP", "subject to L/C opening", "subject to shipping space").
- Currency of price ≠ currency of payment without an exchange-rate clause.
- Total value not written in words / inconsistent with unit price × quantity.
- Quantity tolerance without stating whose option.
- Quality via a standard with no version/grade; via samples without stating who keeps the sample.
- Payment method without banks/governing rules (UCP 600)/bank charges.
- Claim clause without a claim period or required documents.
- Penalty without a cap.
- Arbitration without applicable law/place.
- Force majeure without notification duties or a resolution method.
- Inconsistency between delivery and documents terms, e.g., **FOB but a B/L "Freight Prepaid" is required** → under FOB the buyer pays the main freight, so the B/L should normally read "Freight Collect" (case-question material; [EXT] reasoning based on the FOB obligations in §3.8).

## C7. EXPORT AND IMPORT DOCUMENTS

**Key points:** documents frequently used in export-import transactions; common errors in documents.

### 7.1 Shipping documents (8)
Invoice; Bill of Lading; Certificate of Quantity; Certificate of Quality; Packing List; Certificate of Insurance / Insurance Policy; Certificate of Origin; Inspection Certificate.

### 7.2 Documents frequently used (11)
1 Commercial invoice; 2 Pro forma invoice; 3 Bill of lading; 4 Air waybill; 5 Certificate of origin; 6 Inspection certificate; 7 Insurance certificate; 8 Destination control statement; 9 Shipper's export declaration; 10 Export packing list; 11 Manifest.

### 7.3 Commercial invoice
- A document **issued by the seller to the buyer** detailing the goods sold and the transaction.
- Main contents: description; quantity & unit; price; delivery; payment terms; order date & number; shipper/seller; consignee/buyer; other information.
- Uses: the **buyer** needs it to clear customs, **prove ownership**, and arrange payment; **customs** uses it (with other documents) to determine **customs value** and assess duties & taxes.
- **Checking the commercial invoice against the L/C (7):** (1) issued by the beneficiary named in the L/C? (2) same currency as the L/C? (3) description of goods corresponds with the L/C? (4) quantity complies, incl. tolerance? (5) unit price & total amount comply? (6) delivery terms/Incoterms consistent with the L/C (if required)? (7) marks, numbers and other details consistent with the L/C and other documents?

### 7.4 Pro forma invoice
- A **preliminary/provisional** document issued by the seller to a **prospective** buyer, usually **before** the actual sale and shipment. Information: description, quantity, price & currency, delivery terms/Incoterms, expected shipment time, payment terms.
- Uses: as a preliminary invoice accompanying a quotation; for customs purposes for **non-commercial shipments (samples, advertising materials)**; to support applications for an **import permit or foreign exchange approval**; to give the buyer information to arrange the transaction before shipment.

| Pro forma invoice | Commercial invoice |
|---|---|
| Preliminary / provisional | Final commercial document |
| Usually before the actual sale/shipment | Issued for the actual transaction/shipment |
| Mainly for quotation and preliminary/import procedures | Used for customs valuation, payment, import procedures |
| **Not used for payment** | Represents the actual commercial transaction |

### 7.5 Bill of Lading (B/L)
- A **transport document issued by the carrier to the shipper** (seller, exporter, freight forwarder), confirming receipt of the goods and setting out the terms of carriage.
- **3 functions:** (1) **Receipt for shipment**; (2) **Evidence of the contract of carriage**; (3) **Document of title** – a negotiable B/L lets the holder claim/control the goods (subject to law and the B/L terms).
- **7 types:**
  1. **Clean B/L** – no clause/notation indicating defective goods/packaging.
  2. **Unclean (claused/dirty) B/L** – has such a notation (e.g., bad smell, damaged packaging).
  3. **Shipped on board B/L** – confirms goods loaded on board.
  4. **Received for shipment B/L** – carrier has received the goods, not yet loaded.
  5. **B/L to order** – **negotiable**, transferable by **endorsement and delivery**: to order of the issuing bank / the applicant / the shipper / "to order".
  6. **Straight B/L** – consigned to a **specifically named consignee**.
  7. **Bearer B/L** – deliverable to the holder/bearer.
- **B/L checklist (19):** 1 ship name; 2 port of loading & discharge; 3 place & date of issue; 4 issuer (carrier/agent of carrier/master/agent of master); 5 signature; 6 shipped-on-board date; 7 number of originals; 8 clean on board; 9 L/C number on the B/L; 10 order party (buyer); 11 shipper name/address; 12 consignee name/address; 13 notify party name/address; 14 shipping marks; 15 number of packages; 16 description of goods; 17 weight; 18 freight: **Freight Prepaid or Freight Collect/Payable at Destination**; 19 additional notations (L/C).

### 7.6 Insurance documents
- **Insurance Certificate:** issued by an insurance company or its authorized agent, stating the type and amount of cover and other details.
- **Insurance Policy:** sets out the terms of the insurance contract: general conditions; insurer's responsibilities/liabilities; type & amount of cover; scope; premium.
- **Insurance documents checklist (4):** (1) all required **originals** presented as stipulated in the L/C? (2) issued by an acceptable insurer or authorized agent? → **cover notes issued by brokers are not acceptable under UCP 600**; (3) correctly dated and signed? (4) **dated no later than the shipment date** (loading on board, dispatch, or taking in charge).
- [EXT] UCP 600 Art. 28(c) (cover notes not accepted); Art. 28(e) (date no later than shipment, unless the document shows cover effective from no later than the shipment date); Art. 28(f)(ii) (minimum 110% of the CIF/CIP value if the L/C is silent).

### 7.7 Certificate of Quantity/Weight & Certificate of Quality
- **Quantity/Weight:** certifies quantity/weight as per the contract. **Quality:** certifies quality & specifications comply with the contract.
- Issued by: the **producer/seller or an independent inspection/supervisory organization**, as required by the contract.

### 7.8 Certificate of Origin (C/O)
- Certifies the **country of origin** of exported goods; depending on the trade agreement, used to determine eligibility for **preferential tariff treatment**.
- **Main forms:** Form A – GSP; **Form B – non-preferential**; Form D – ATIGA (ASEAN); Form E – ACFTA (ASEAN–China); Form AK – ASEAN–Korea; Form AJ – ASEAN–Japan; Form AI – ASEAN–India; Form AANZ – ASEAN–Australia–New Zealand; Form RCEP; Form VK – Vietnam–Korea FTA; Form VJ – Vietnam–Japan EPA; **Form EUR.1 – EVFTA**; Form EUR.1 UK – UKVFTA; Form CPTPP.
- **CPTPP:** 12 members (list per slide: Vietnam, Australia, Brunei, Canada, Malaysia, Mexico, New Zealand, Peru, Singapore, Japan, United Kingdom) – [ERRATA E-08].
- **RCEP:** 15 members = 10 ASEAN + China, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand.
- **GSP (Generalized System of Preferences):** a multilateral policy under **UNCTAD** whereby **developed countries** grant preferential tariffs (exemption or reduction) to goods from **developing and least-developed countries**. Purpose: reduce tariffs to the MFN level, or exempt tariffs for certain products from preference-receiving countries.

### 7.9 Other documents
- **Packing list:** prepared by the **shipper**, listing kinds and quantities of goods in a shipment; a copy is sent to the consignee to help check the shipment on arrival.
- **Phytosanitary inspection certificate** (kiểm dịch thực vật): issued by a **government authority** to meet the importing country's regulations, confirming the shipment was inspected and is **free from harmful pests and plant diseases**.
- **Inspection certificate** (giám định): signed by the **seller/exporter or an independent inspection firm** (as required by the buyer), confirming the goods meet certain specifications.

## C8. EXPORT & IMPORT PROCEDURES

### 8.1 Key considerations in implementing contracts
1. Terms of sale – Incoterms®; 2. Method of payment. (These two decide which steps each party performs.)

### 8.2 Export procedures (10 steps)
1. **Negotiate and sign the sales contract** – agree on price, quantity, quality, delivery terms, payment terms, etc.
2. **Consider payment terms** – per the agreed terms (T/T, L/C, D/P…); **check the L/C if payment is by L/C**.
3. **Prepare goods for export** – packing, labeling, marking, securing export documents.
4. **Inspect goods** – if required by the contract, L/C or regulations.
5. **Arrange transportation** – per the Incoterms rule; **the seller books main carriage under C & D rules**.
6. **Arrange cargo insurance** – if required by the contract or the rule (**CIF & CIP**).
7. **Complete export customs clearance** – prepare & submit documents, pay duties/taxes (if applicable).
8. **Deliver the goods** – per the Incoterms rule (at port, at destination…).
9. **Prepare and present shipping documents for payment** – as required by the payment method (L/C, D/P, T/T…).
10. **Handle claims and resolve disputes** – if any (damaged goods, shortage, late delivery…).
- **Notes:** an export license is **not always required** (only when the goods are regulated); transport and insurance depend on the Incoterms rule; "delivery" means the time and place defined by the Incoterms rule; presenting documents for payment depends on the payment method.

### 8.3 Import procedures (9 steps)
1. **Negotiate & sign the sale contract** with the supplier.
2. **Consider payment terms** – **open the L/C if payment is by L/C**.
3. **Arrange transportation** – **the buyer books main carriage under E & F rules**.
4. **Arrange cargo insurance** – if any: under **E, F, CPT, CFR** (rules where the seller is not obliged to insure while the buyer bears risk in main carriage).
5. **Complete import customs clearance** – submit the declaration, provide documents, pay duties & fees.
6. **Take delivery of goods** – receive from the carrier/terminal and arrange inland transport.
7. **Inspect goods** – check quantity, quality, condition and conformity with the contract.
8. **Claim & settle claims** – with the seller, carrier or insurer for losses/discrepancies.
9. **Make payment** – per the agreed payment terms.
